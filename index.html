01 / The formal results

Agreement has conditions.

Existence, convergence, uniqueness and average balance are distinct conclusions. Follow each one to its assumptions and Lean source.

Definitions 1–3Foundation

A language for local repair

Typed node states, neighbourhoods and evidence define a global repair map. Equilibrium, surprise and detuning become explicit predicates and quantities.

Theorem 4Existence

An agreement exists

Nonempty compact convex node domains and a continuous global repair map give an equilibrium. A four-module Brouwer development supplies the fixed-point argument.

Theorems 5 & 12Contraction

Repair reaches it

A contraction gives a unique equilibrium and quantitative convergence bounds under the stated domain hypotheses. Disagreement bounds displacement by D/(1 + q) and D/(1 − q).

Theorem 6Potential

A shared quantity decreases

For cyclic repair with unique coordinate minimizers of a continuous potential on compact domains, the potential never increases and every limit point is an equilibrium.

Proposition 7Partial coverage

Existence can outlast reachability

The clipped two-node map has one equilibrium, yet other synchronous starts do not converge. Relaxation nonconvergence and square invariance are included; the periodic-orbit claim remains open here.

Balance while states move

Bounded relaxation yields a difference between average state and average repair target of at most diam(X)/T. Further results cover affine repair, rate laws and integrated charge balance.

Two equilibria give a gradient

The equilibrium-propagation identity connects free and nudged stationary states under smoothness and a differentiable stationary branch. Related results address pairwise energy and two timescales.

02 / The boundary of the claim

Read the hypotheses, too.

The formalization is strongest when its precise statements and its remaining gaps are equally easy to find.

Explicit conditions

  • The continuous contraction bound assumes the trajectory stays in the domain.
  • Average-balance and rate-law results include continuity assumptions.
  • The spiking charge-balance result uses an integrated formulation.
  • Equilibrium limit points under a potential do not guarantee convergence of the whole sequence to one point.

Outside this formalization

  • The periodic-orbit conclusion in Proposition 7.
  • The numerical simulations in Section 6, including the fly-brain experiment.
  • Externally cited results: asynchronous convergence, Kakutani, Nash, Arrow–Debreu, Aumann, DeGroot, sheaf heat flow and OPH reconstruction.

The proofs address consequences of encoded mathematical hypotheses. Applying them to physical or cognitive systems requires a justified model and independent evidence. The paper's universality thesis remains a scientific thesis. Full result map and caveats ↗
